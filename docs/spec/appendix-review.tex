\subsection{Version 2.6.2: recovery freshness and entry approvals}
Version 2.6.2 corrects 2.6.1 after code review v3, without adding or removing a requirement. \rr{AVL-008} now states what happens when the restored catalog keeps no record of how far the separately protected recovery record reached, as after a restore onto an empty catalog: nothing local shows that the record holds every change after the backup, so admission reopens only after an explicit, audited operator statement that it is complete, or an explicit acceptance of its loss. This reconciles G-03 with N-08. The resource type table gives \code{text.entry} the \code{approve} action, so a grant on a collection entry can wait for an approval like one on a unit. Case V-179 is added.

\subsection{Version 2.6.1: share-grant targets}
Version 2.6.1 corrects 2.6 without adding or removing a requirement. \rr{SHR-001} names buckets, collection entries and derived outputs as grant targets and requires \code{share} on them, but the resource type table gave those types no \code{share} action, and its grantable actions (\code{read}, \code{stat}, \code{discover}) fit objects only. The table now lists \code{share} for \code{storage.bucket}, \code{text.entry} and \code{document.derived}, and a grant's actions come from its target type's read set: \code{read}, \code{stat} and \code{discover} on an object; \code{read-units} and \code{discover} on a document version; \code{read} on a unit or entry; \code{deliver} on a derived output; \code{list} on a bucket.

\subsection{Design decisions retained from earlier revisions}
R1 delivers encrypted object storage and shared fine-grained authorization; document units are storage resources. R2 adds MCP as a separate client of those services, with model or external-tool execution separately enabled. S3 and custom replication remain R3 extensions. Every payload is encrypted. Since 2.6 the platform holds server-mode keys and releases them only under decisions; in client mode clients alone hold payload keys and decrypt, and the server stores ciphertext, opaque recipient envelopes and declared routing/policy metadata.

The independently authored Go evaluator uses qualified general-purpose expression and cryptographic libraries. Upstream MinIO and Cerbos implementations are research references, not production dependencies. A matching deny overrides an allow, and always-run platform ceilings stay distinct from business policy. Our own single-node blob store is the R1 backend (D-02); publication requires durable bytes and an authorized catalog transition. External effects retain explicit UNKNOWN and ABANDONED outcomes. Client retention, malicious-client lineage and unmediated egress keep their stated limits.

The detailed historical review responses remain in the preceding editions. Current requirement text governs; obsolete server-custody and upstream-runtime alternatives are not revived by historical mapping notes.
